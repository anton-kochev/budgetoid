# The Problem Budgetoid Solves

Budgetoid grew out of a familiar experience: money kept running out at exactly the moment an
unavoidable expense arrived — the annual insurance, the car service, the planned thing that was
simply forgotten. Saving toward anything big, like a vacation, stayed theoretical: the sum was
knowable and could have been accumulated calmly over months, but no tool made that convenient.
Budgeting changed that — spending became calm, plans fit within means, and for the first time money
was left over to set aside and invest. Budgetoid exists to be the tool that makes that change easy.

**Budgetoid exists so that money is assigned to its purposes before it is spent — so the inevitable
never surprises you, big goals actually get funded, and "can I afford this?" always has a plain
answer.**

The product credo, in five words: **money gets its jobs first.** All of it — nothing stays
unassigned, and saving and investing are jobs too.

## Mission

Help people decide what their money is for — before they spend it. When all of your money has a
purpose, bills stop being surprises, goals get funded month by month, and "can I afford this?"
always has an answer. The real outcome is bigger than a budget: peace of mind — and capital that
grows, calmly and steadily.

## The three problems

### 1. The inevitable, unassigned

The expenses that break a month are rarely surprises — they are inevitable but unassigned. No money
was set aside for them, so they land on whatever is left, and whatever is left is never enough.
Budgetoid lets these obligations be planned and reserved for, month by month, before they arrive.

Even surprises can be budgeted and become expected.

### 2. The unfunded goal

Vacation-sized goals need gradual accumulation, and a bank account gives no structure for it.
Without a place to put "vacation money," it is eaten by ordinary days. Budgetoid gives savings a
named purpose and a steady path toward it.

### 3. The affordability question

At the moment of spending, the honest question is "can I afford this — right now?" An account
balance cannot answer it, because part of that balance already belongs to the future. When every
part of your money has a job, the answer comes from the job the spending would draw on — what is
left in Clothing decides the jacket, plainly and immediately.

## The foundation

Two things make the model work:

- **The honest record.** Every transaction is entered by hand, deliberately — the moment of writing
  it down is where awareness comes from. Entry must therefore cost seconds, or it will not happen.
- **The single picture.** Every account — bank, card, cash — in one place, one total, one truth
  about what is where.

## The principle

**Plain answers, never judgment.** Every screen answers its question at a glance, in words a person
uses. The system never says money was spent badly; it only says, plainly, what is still free to
spend.

## The outcome

Calm, first of all — money stops being a background worry. Spending without stress, because
affordability is a known fact, not a gamble; planning within means; and capital that grows steadily
— saving stops being an accident.

## Product consequences

- The mental model is **allocation-first**: give money a purpose before spending it. Recording and
  reviewing serve allocation, not the other way around.
- The central number everywhere is **available within a job** — not "spent" and not account
  balance. Nothing stays unassigned: what is left after obligations gets a job too, and saving and
  investing are jobs.
- Obligations and goals (reserved amounts that accumulate over months) are the missing domain layer
  and will become first-class concepts; the current model (accounts, transactions, categories) is
  the recording foundation they will build on.
- Adding a transaction is the flagship interaction: pre-filled date, remembered account, payee
  autocomplete, amount first — a few seconds end to end.
- Budgetoid serves a real audience from day one: empty states, first-run experience, and
  multi-currency are core, not afterthoughts.
- The user only and always owns their data. Full export and complete erasure are core features the
  product promises publicly — they must exist and stay one click away, not buried. The full privacy
  stance is held back until the product keeps it: it lives in `product-research/privacy.md` in the
  private `budgetoid-specs` repository, and returns here the day it describes shipped behaviour
  rather than intent.
